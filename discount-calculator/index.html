How a discount works

A discount of 25% takes a quarter off the price, so you pay the other 75%. The quickest way to the sale price is to multiply the original price by what is left. That is 1 minus the discount as a decimal.

sale price     = original x (1 - discount / 100)
you save       = original - sale price
percent off    = (original - sale) / original x 100
original price = sale price / (1 - discount / 100)

How to use the calculator

  1. Type the original price and the discount. The sale price and the saving appear straight away.
  2. If there is a second discount, such as a coupon on top of a sale, type it in the extra discount box. The calculator applies it to the already reduced price.
  3. If you only know the two prices, use the next box to see the percent off and the saving. If you know the price you paid and the discount, the last box finds the original price.

Worked example: $80 at 25% off. 80 x 0.75 = $60, so the saving is $20.

A common mistake is to add the discount to the price paid: 25% of 60 is 15, but the original price is not 75. The 25% was taken from the original, so the original is 60 / 0.75 = 80.

Discount and sales tax

Both are multipliers, so the order does not change the final total: 80 x 0.75 x 1.08 = 64.8. The tax amount does change, because it is charged on a smaller price if the discount comes first. The calculator does not add tax; take the sale price it gives and multiply by 1 plus your rate.

What this calculator leaves out

It works on the price you type. It does not add sales tax or delivery, does not know about minimum spend or coupon rules, and shows results to two decimal places, so a shop's till may round a cent differently. It does not compare unit prices, such as price per kilogram, which is the fair way to compare two pack sizes. For a rise rather than a fall, use the percentage increase calculator. The guide to percentage formulas covers the common mistakes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a sale price?

Multiply the original price by 1 minus the discount as a decimal. An $80 item at 25% off costs 80 x 0.75 = $60, so you save $20.

How do I find the percent off from two prices?

Subtract the sale price from the original, divide by the original, and multiply by 100. From $120 down to $90 is (120 - 90) / 120 x 100 = 25% off.

What do two discounts in a row add up to?

They multiply, they do not add. 20% off and then a further 10% off leaves 0.80 x 0.90 = 0.72 of the price, which is 28% off in total, not 30%. Use the extra discount box to see it.

How do I work out the original price from the sale price?

Divide the sale price by 1 minus the discount. If an item costs $60 after 25% off, the original price was 60 / 0.75 = $80. The calculator has a box for this.

How much is 20% off?

Take 20% of the price and subtract it, or multiply by 0.80. 20% off $50 is $40, a saving of $10. The table above lists the sale price at other common discounts.

Is it better to have a discount before or after tax?

The total is the same either way when both are percentages. $80 at 25% off and 8% tax is $64.8 whichever comes first. What differs is the amount of tax: tax on the sale price is lower than tax on the full price. Which one applies depends on how the shop or the tax rules treat the discount.

Does it include sales tax?

No. The calculator works on the price you enter. Sales tax is usually added after the discount, so multiply the sale price by 1 plus your tax rate. With 8% tax, $60 becomes $64.8.

What is buy one get one 50% off really worth?

On two items at the same price it is a 25% discount on the pair. For two $40 items you pay 40 + 20 = $60 instead of $80, which is $20 off, or 25%. Enter 80 and 60 in the two-price box to check.

Is 30% off and 20% off the same as 50% off?

No. Taken one after the other, they leave 56% of the price, so the discount is 44%. 50% off would leave 50%.

Can I use it to find a markup or a price increase?

Not directly. A markup goes up from the original price, so use the percentage increase calculator. It uses the same method with 1 plus the percentage instead of 1 minus it.
