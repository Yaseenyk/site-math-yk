Percentage formulas explained: of, is-what-percent and percent change

"Percent" means per hundred, so 15% is 15 out of every 100, or 0.15. Almost every percentage question is one of the five below. The percentage calculator does each of them with your own numbers.

The five formulas

X% of Y              = X / 100 x Y          15% of 80  = 12
X is what % of Y     = X / Y x 100          12 of 80   = 15%
X is Y% of what      = X / (Y / 100)        12 is 15%  -> 80
percent change       = (new - old) / old x 100       40 -> 50 = +25%
new after Y% change  = old x (1 +/- Y / 100)         80 +25%  = 100

Mistake 1: percent versus percentage points

If an interest rate rises from 4% to 5%, it has risen by 1 percentage point. As a percent change it is up 25%, because 1 is a quarter of 4. News stories mix the two, so check which one is meant.

Mistake 2: a rise and a fall do not cancel

A percentage always applies to the current value. 100 falling by 50% is 50, and 50 rising by 50% is only 75. To get back to 100 from 50 you need a rise of 100%. This is why a share price that halves has to double to recover.

Mistake 3: adding discounts

Two discounts multiply. 20% off and then 10% off leaves 0.80 x 0.90 = 0.72 of the price, which is 28% off, not 30%. The percentage decrease calculator shows the result for one step at a time.

Mistake 4: using the wrong base

Percent change is measured against the starting value. From 40 to 50 is +25%, but from 50 to 40 is -20%. If neither value is the starting point, use percent difference, which divides by the average and gives the same answer both ways: 22.22% for 40 and 50. The percent change calculator shows both.

Mental shortcuts

  • 10% of a number: move the decimal point one place left.
  • 5%: half of 10%. 1%: move the decimal point two places.
  • 15% is 10% plus 5%. 25% is a quarter. 50% is half.

Worked example: a price with a discount and tax

An item is listed at 1,000. It is on sale at 20% off, and 18% tax is added at the till. The order matters for how you describe it but not for the result of two multiplications: 1,000 x 0.80 = 800 after the discount, then 800 x 1.18 = 944 with tax. Doing it the other way round, 1,000 x 1.18 x 0.80, is also 944, because multiplication can be done in either order. What is not the same is adding the percentages: 1,000 x (1 + 0.18 - 0.20) would be 980, which is wrong.

Working backwards from a total

A bill is 1,180 and it includes 18% tax. The tax is not 18% of 1,180. The pre-tax price is 1,180 / 1.18 = 1,000, so the tax is 180. Taking 18% of 1,180 gives 212.40, which is the classic wrong answer, because 18% applies to the price before tax, not to the total. The same rule finds the original price before any known percentage change: divide by (1 + the change).

Percent change in a row

Successive changes multiply. Up 10% and then down 10% is 100 x 1.10 x 0.90 = 99, a net fall of 1%, not zero. Three years of 5% growth is 1.05 x 1.05 x 1.05 = 1.157625, so 15.76% in total, slightly more than 3 x 5% = 15%, because each year's growth is on a larger base. The percentage increase calculator does one step; chain the results for several.

When a percentage is not enough

  • A percentage of a very small base looks dramatic: a rise from 2 to 4 cases is up 100%, but 2 more cases. Quote the counts as well.
  • Percentages of different groups cannot be added or averaged without weighting them. If 50% of 10 people and 20% of 90 people agree, the combined figure is (5 + 18) / 100 = 23%, not 35%.
  • Percent difference and percent change answer different questions. Use change when one value is the starting point and difference when neither is.

All of the formulas on this page are standard arithmetic definitions of percentage and need no external source; each example above can be checked with the calculators linked on this page or with any ordinary calculator.
