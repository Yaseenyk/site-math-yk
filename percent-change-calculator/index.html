The formulas

percent change     = (new - old) / |old| x 100
percent difference = |a - b| / ((a + b) / 2) x 100

Worked example

From 40 to 50: the change is 50 - 40 = 10, and 10 / 40 x 100 = +25%. From 50 back to 40 it is -10 / 50 x 100 = -20%. The percent difference between 40 and 50 is 10 / 45 x 100 = 22.22% either way, because the base is the average, 45.

How to calculate percent change

  1. Subtract the old value from the new value. The sign tells you the direction.
  2. Divide by the old value, ignoring its sign. The old value is always the base.
  3. Multiply by 100 and add a percent sign. Positive is an increase, negative a decrease.

The same move in both directions

Going up and coming back are not equal percentages, because each is measured against its own starting value. The percent difference is shown for comparison, since it gives one answer for both.

Which one should I use?

  • Percent change: when one value is clearly the starting point, such as last year's sales and this year's.
  • Percent difference: when you compare two values on equal footing, such as two measurements of the same thing.

What this calculator leaves out

It compares two values. It does not chain several periods into growth over time; for that, multiply by 1 plus the rate for each period, as the percentage increase calculator page shows. When the old value is zero there is no percent change, and when it is negative or near zero the result is hard to interpret, so look at the absolute change as well. Frequently asked questions

What is percent change?

How much a value moved, as a percentage of where it started: (new - old) / old x 100. A positive result is an increase and a negative result is a decrease.

What is the difference between percent change and percent difference?

Percent change has a direction and uses the old value as the base. Percent difference compares two values with neither as the starting point, using their average as the base, so it comes out the same whichever is first.

Why does 40 to 50 give +25% but 50 to 40 gives -20%?

Each is measured against its own starting value: 10 is 25% of 40, but only 20% of 50. Percent difference avoids this: both directions give 22.22%.

What happens if the old value is zero?

The percent change is undefined, because you cannot divide by zero. The calculator asks for a non-zero old value.

How do I use it for negative numbers?

The formula divides by the size of the old value, so a move from -50 to -25 is shown as a +50% change. For quantities that can cross zero, absolute change is often clearer than a percentage.

How do I calculate percent change in a spreadsheet?

With the old value in A1 and the new value in B1, enter =(B1-A1)/ABS(A1) and format the cell as a percentage.

How do I calculate percent change step by step?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100. For 62 to 78 the change is 16, and 16 / 62 x 100 = 25.81%.

Why does a move of the same size give different percentages?

Because the base is different. A rise of 10 is +100% from 10, +10% from 100 and +1% from 1,000.

Is a 10% change the same as 10 percentage points?

No. Moving from 40% to 50% is 10 percentage points, but a 25% change, because 10 / 40 x 100 = 25.
