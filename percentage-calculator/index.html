Common percentages as fractions

50% is 1/2, 25% is 1/4, 20% is 1/5, 12.5% is 1/8, 10% is 1/10 and 5% is 1/20. If you spot one of these, divide instead of multiplying: 25% of 80 is 80 / 4 = 20.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Stacking percentages by adding them. A 10% rise followed by another 10% is 21%, not 20%, because the second is applied to the larger number.
  • Using the wrong base. In "X is what percent of Y" the total Y is the denominator. 20 is 25% of 80 but 80 is 400% of 20.
  • Mixing percent and percentage points. See the guide to percentage formulas.

A quick way to do it in your head

Ten percent of a number is the number with the decimal point moved one place left: 10% of 80 is 8. Five percent is half of that, 4. So 15% of 80 is 8 + 4 = 12. One percent is the decimal point moved two places.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate X% of a number?

Divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by the number. 15% of 80 is 15 / 100 x 80 = 12.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

Divide the first number by the second and multiply by 100. 12 is 12 / 80 x 100 = 15% of 80.

How do I find the whole number when I know a part and the percentage?

Divide the part by the percentage as a decimal. If 12 is 15% of a number, the number is 12 / 0.15 = 80.

How do I calculate a percentage of a percentage?

Multiply them as decimals. 20% of 50% is 0.20 x 0.50 = 0.10, which is 10%. Use the first calculator and enter 20 as the percentage and 50 as the number.

How do I turn a percentage into a decimal?

Divide by 100, which moves the decimal point two places left. 15% is 0.15, and 7.5% is 0.075.

How do I work out a test score as a percentage?

Divide the marks you got by the total marks and multiply by 100. 42 out of 60 is 42 / 60 x 100 = 70%.

Why does adding 10% twice not give 20%?

The second 10% is taken from the already-increased number. 100 up 10% is 110, and 110 up 10% is 121, which is a 21% total rise, not 20%.

What is the difference between a percentage and a percentage point?

A percentage point is a plain subtraction of two percentages. A rate moving from 10% to 15% is up 5 percentage points, which is a 50% relative increase (5 / 10 x 100).
