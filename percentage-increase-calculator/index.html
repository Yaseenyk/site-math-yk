The formulas

percentage increase = (new - old) / old x 100
new value          = old x (1 + increase / 100)

How to calculate a percentage increase

  1. Subtract the old value from the new value to get the amount of the rise.
  2. Divide that amount by the old value, not the new one.
  3. Multiply by 100 to make it a percentage.

Worked example: 50 to 65. The rise is 15, 15 / 50 = 0.3, so the increase is 30%. Going the other way, to raise 80 by 25% you multiply by 1.25 and get 100.

To raise a number by a percentage, multiply by 1 plus the percentage. The last column shows the fall that would undo the rise.

After a rise of 100% the value has doubled, and a fall of 50% takes it back. That is why a percentage rise and the same percentage fall do not cancel.

Percentage increase or percent change?

They use the same formula. A positive answer is an increase and a negative one a decrease, which the percent change calculator labels for you. If neither value is the starting point, use the percentage difference calculator.

What this calculator leaves out

It compares two values, or applies one rate once. It does not add interest by the month, tax, or inflation adjustments, and the yearly table above assumes the same rate every year. When the old value is zero there is no percentage increase, because the base would be zero. A rise from a very small number gives a huge percentage that says little, so check the amount of the rise too.

Prices, salaries, rents and populations are all compared this way. For a pay rise specifically, use the pay raise calculator. For a fall, see the percentage decrease calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percentage increase?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100. From 50 to 65 is (65 - 50) / 50 x 100 = 30%.

How do I increase a number by a percentage?

Multiply it by 1 plus the percentage as a decimal. Increasing 80 by 25% is 80 x 1.25 = 100.

Why is going from 50 to 100 a 100% increase but 100 to 50 only a 50% decrease?

Percent change is measured against the starting value. Doubling adds 100% of 50. Halving removes 50% of 100. The same gap of 50 is a different share of a different starting point.

What is the difference between a percent and a percentage point?

A rate rising from 4% to 5% is a rise of 1 percentage point, but a 25% increase, because 1 is a quarter of 4.

Can a percentage increase be more than 100%?

Yes. Any new value more than double the old value is an increase over 100%. From 20 to 70 is (70 - 20) / 20 x 100 = 250%.

How do I find the original number before a percentage increase?

Divide the new value by 1 plus the percentage as a decimal. If a price is 125 after a 25% rise, the original was 125 / 1.25 = 100.

What happens if a number goes up 20% and then down 20%?

It ends lower than it started. 100 up 20% is 120, and 120 down 20% is 96, a net fall of 4%, because the 20% drop is taken from the bigger number.

How do I work out growth over several years?

Multiply by 1 plus the rate once per year. Three years of 5% growth is 1.05 x 1.05 x 1.05 = 1.157625, a total increase of about 15.76%, not 15%.
