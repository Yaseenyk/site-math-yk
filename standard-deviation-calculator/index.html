What standard deviation tells you

Standard deviation is the typical distance of the values in a list from their mean. Two lists can have the same mean and very different spread: 48, 50, 52 and 30, 50, 70 both average 50, but their sample standard deviations are 2 and 20. The mean alone hides that difference.

The formulas

mean                 = sum / n
population variance  = sum of (x - mean)^2 / n
sample variance      = sum of (x - mean)^2 / (n - 1)
standard deviation   = square root of the variance

The sample formula, with N - 1 in the denominator, is the one NIST gives for the standard deviation of a sample (see Sources).

How to calculate it, step by step

  1. Add the numbers and divide by how many there are. That is the mean.
  2. Subtract the mean from each number. Square each result so that negatives do not cancel.
  3. Add the squares.
  4. Divide by n for a whole group, or by n - 1 for a sample. That is the variance.
  5. Take the square root. That is the standard deviation.

Worked example

For 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9, the mean is 40 / 8 = 5.

Sample or population: how much does it matter?

The sample figure is the population figure multiplied by the square root of n / (n - 1). The gap is large for tiny lists and shrinks as the list grows.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Using the population figure for a small sample. With 8 values it understates the spread by about 6.5%.
  • Reading variance as if it were in your units. A variance of 4 for numbers in metres is in square metres. Take the square root to get back to metres.
  • Comparing standard deviations of data on different scales. A spread of 5 means different things for heights in centimetres and for incomes.
  • Leaving out a value because it looks odd. One extreme value can change the result a lot.

What this calculator leaves out

It takes a plain list of values. It does not accept frequency tables or weights, does not give a standard error or a confidence interval, and does not flag outliers. It also does not tell you whether your data is a sample or the whole group; that depends on how it was collected. For the mean, median and mode of the same list, use the average calculator. To compare two values rather than describe a list, see the percent change calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is standard deviation?

A measure of how spread out numbers are around their mean. A small value means the numbers cluster near the mean; a large value means they are widely scattered. It is in the same units as your data.

What is the difference between sample and population standard deviation?

Use the population version when your numbers are the whole group you care about, and divide by n. Use the sample version when they are only a sample of a larger group, and divide by n - 1, which corrects for a sample underestimating the spread.

Which one should I use?

If you measured every member of the group, such as the marks of every student in one class you are describing, use population. If the numbers are a sample used to learn about a bigger group, such as 30 customers out of thousands, use sample. When unsure, sample is the usual choice.

How do I calculate standard deviation by hand?

Find the mean, subtract it from each number and square the result, add the squares, divide by n (population) or n - 1 (sample), and take the square root. For 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 the mean is 5, the squares add up to 32, and the population standard deviation is the square root of 32 / 8, which is 2.

What is variance?

The standard deviation before the square root: the average of the squared differences from the mean. Standard deviation is easier to read because it is in the same units as your data, while variance is in squared units.

Why divide by n - 1 for a sample?

The deviations are measured from the sample's own mean, which sits closer to the sample values than the true mean of the whole group would. That makes the spread come out a little too small, and dividing by n - 1 instead of n compensates. The size of the effect fades as the sample grows; see the table above.

Why does the sample version need at least two values?

Dividing by n - 1 with one value means dividing by zero. One value has no spread to estimate, so the sample result is not defined and the calculator shows needs 2+ values.

Can the standard deviation be negative, or zero?

It cannot be negative, because it is a square root of an average of squares. It is zero only when every value is identical, such as 7, 7, 7.

Is a standard deviation of 10 big or small?

It depends on the data. Compare it with the mean and the units. A spread of 10 around a mean of 1,000 is small. A spread of 10 around a mean of 20 is large. The two lists 48, 50, 52 and 30, 50, 70 share the mean 50 but have sample standard deviations of 2 and 20.

What does the calculator do with text in my list?

Numbers can be separated by commas, spaces, semicolons or new lines. Anything that is not a number is ignored and listed above the result, so a stray word will not change the answer silently.
